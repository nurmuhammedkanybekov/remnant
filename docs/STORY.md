# REMNANT — Story Bible

The canonical reference for REMNANT's world, characters and campaign. Level
text (notes, radio lines, objectives) should be consistent with this file.

---

## Premise

**Object 9 "Zenit"** was a Soviet deep-drilling research station cut into the
Tian Shan mountains in 1961. Officially it studied geothermal energy.
Unofficially, at 2.4 km down, the drill broke into a cavity lined with
something that was neither rock nor tissue — a slow, warm, crystalline mass
the researchers named **the Remnant**.

The Remnant does not kill. It _rewrites_. Anything that touches it for long
enough is folded into it: muscle becomes cable, eyes grow over, bones lengthen
into tools. The station was sealed in 1991 and forgotten.

In the present day, a private mining consortium reopened Zenit to strip it for
rare earth metals. Forty-one contractors went down. Eleven days ago the
shafts collapsed.

**You** are one of three survivors of the consortium's crew, chosen when a
game starts: **Nur Kanybekov**, a structural engineer on the survey team;
**Raiymbek Asanov**, a drilling foreman; or **Nuraiza Akylbek**, a field
geologist. The script is written for Nur, and every line, note and subtitle
is told to whoever is playing (`personalise` in `content/characters.ts`). You wake up in the infirmary on **Sublevel 10**, the deepest level, with
a head wound, a pistol that isn't yours and a flashlight with a dying battery.
The only thing that works is a wall radio, and there's a voice on it.

## The creatures

All creatures are former people (or animals) rewritten by the Remnant. They are
**blind or nearly blind** — the Remnant grows over the eyes first — so they
hunt by **sound** and by **heat/light**. This is the core of the gameplay:
noise and the flashlight are what give you away.

| Creature        | Gameplay                                                                   | Lore                                                                                    |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Husk**        | Fast, fragile hunter.                                                      | The most common result of exposure. Still walks upright, still remembers the corridors. |
| **Brute**       | Slow, tanky, hits hard.                                                    | Several people fused into one frame.                                                    |
| **Listener**    | Completely blind, very sensitive hearing; ignores the flashlight entirely. | Its skull has opened into a resonating chamber.                                         |
| **Watcher**     | Only moves when your flashlight is _not_ on it. Freezes in light.          | Its skin is photosensitive; light physically locks its muscles.                         |
| **Crawler**     | Clings to ceilings and drops on you.                                       | Rewritten maintenance workers who lived in the vents.                                   |
| **Spitter**     | Keeps its distance and spits corrosive fluid.                              | Its lungs became something else.                                                        |
| **Swarm**       | Many small, weak, fast enemies.                                            | Rats. The Remnant doesn't care what it rewrites.                                        |
| **Mimic**       | Imitates sounds (footsteps, pickups, _the Operator's voice_) to lure you.  | The first sign that the Remnant is learning.                                            |
| **The Remnant** | Final boss.                                                                | The mass itself, awake.                                                                 |

## The truth, and how the player learns it

**How it spreads: by voice.** The Remnant learns people by listening to
them and talking to them. Anyone who listens to it long enough is slowly
rewritten: first a hum they can't place, then the smile, then the rest. Its
creatures are what's left of the people it finished with. It keeps every
voice it ever learned, and it can speak in any of them.

**The Operator is the Remnant**, speaking over the facility radio in a
voice assembled from everyone it has absorbed. It needs a living, _unchanged_
host to carry a fragment of it up the shaft: the blast doors at the top were
built in 1991 by Dr. Arkadin to open only for someone unchanged. So it guides
Nur up, and talks to him all the way, a little at a time.

**Why Nur is still Nur.** Mara Voss, the medic, kept Nur sedated in the dark
with the radio unplugged after the collapse. He slept through the eleven
days in which everyone else sat listening.

**Why the consortium came.** Not for rare earth metals: Project GLASS was
to bring one living Zenit sample (R-7) to the surface. The crew were not
told. When it went wrong, security chief Hendricks blew the shafts himself,
so nobody could leave.

**The ending choice.** Arkadin wired the shaft with charges in 1991, for
the day the Remnant learned to wear someone well enough to pass the doors.
At the top, Nur can walk out (and the last scene hints what came with him),
or bring the mountain down.

## Endings

At the top of the lift shaft the blast door opens for you. Nur has a choice:

- **Seal** — Trigger the consortium's self-destruct charges from the lift
  controls. The shaft collapses with Nur inside. The Remnant stays buried.
  _"Daylight was never the point."_
- **Leave** — Walk out into the snow. Roll credits over the mountains. After
  the credits: a short scene of the Operator's radio crackling to life in a
  village clinic in the valley.

Nur climbs from Sublevel 10 to the surface. Each level introduces **one new
idea** so the game keeps teaching until the end.

## Tone and writing rules

- Notes are short (1–3 sentences), written by real people in a hurry. No
  exposition dumps.
- The Operator is calm, warm and helpful — never obviously sinister until
  Level 9.
- The facility is Soviet-era concrete refitted with modern consortium
  equipment: Cyrillic stencils next to corporate safety signs.
- Horror comes from sound and scarcity, not gore.
